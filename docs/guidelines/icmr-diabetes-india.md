# ICMR and Indian Diabetes Risk Score

Source: ICMR public diabetes guidance and Mohan et al., Indian Diabetes Risk Score (JAPI 2005). Educational notes for an Indian screening demo.

India has a high burden of type 2 diabetes, often at younger age and lower BMI than Western cohorts. Screening tools that use age, waist, activity, and family history (IDRS) are meant for awareness, not diagnosis.

Waist circumference cut-offs used in IDRS are 90 cm and 100 cm for men and 80 cm and 90 cm for women. Sedentary work scores highest. Family history in one or both parents adds points.

ICMR-style advice for people at higher IDRS emphasises daily walking, less sitting, a plate with vegetables and whole grains, and a clinic check of fasting glucose or HbA1c. IDRS cannot confirm diabetes. Lab tests and a clinician do that.
