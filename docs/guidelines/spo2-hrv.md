# Oxygen, recovery, and when to seek care

Source: public pulse-oximetry teaching and HRV research summaries. Not a diagnostic protocol.

Adult SpO2 is usually 95% or higher at sea level. Repeated nights with minimum SpO2 under 94% on a consumer wearable can be artefact (loose strap, cold fingers) or a signal to ask a clinician about sleep or lung issues. A single low night is not a diagnosis of sleep apnoea.

HRV (for example RMSSD) is lower with age, acute illness, poor sleep, and heavy alcohol. A drop versus a person’s own baseline is more useful than a population cut-off. HRV apps cannot diagnose anxiety, heart failure, or overtraining.

Emergency signs such as chest pain, sudden breathlessness, fainting, or one-sided weakness need 112 or an emergency department, not a chatbot.
