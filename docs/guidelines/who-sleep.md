# Adult sleep duration

Source: public sleep-duration consensus used by WHO-linked health promotion and AHA lifestyle advice. Short original notes for this prototype.

Most adults do better with 7 to 9 hours of sleep a night. Habitual sleep under 6 hours is linked with higher cardiometabolic risk in observational studies. Very long sleep can also flag other illness and is not a treatment goal.

A regular bedtime, a dark quiet room, and limiting late caffeine and alcohol are first-line sleep hygiene steps. Wearable sleep stages are estimates. They should not be used to diagnose a sleep disorder.

If loud snoring, witnessed pauses in breathing, or very low overnight oxygen appear often, a clinician should review possible sleep-disordered breathing.
